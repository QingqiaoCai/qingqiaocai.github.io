Wearable Microneedle-Based Bioelectronic Platform for Continuous Sensing

Summer 2026
Visiting Researcher · Professor Kaiyu Fu · University of Notre Dame

During my summer research at Notre Dame, I worked on a wearable microneedle-based bioelectronic platform designed for continuous sensing. The system architecture integrates a front-end sensing module with an arm-cuff module for power, computation, communication, and downstream visualization.

My work involved microneedle-related device development and system-level thinking around sensing architecture, signal acquisition, and hardware integration. The project further strengthened my interest in wearable bioelectronics and in building complete biomedical sensing systems rather than isolated components.

Wearable EEG–EOG Monitoring System

Independent Project
System design, hardware implementation, wireless communication, and real-time monitoring

I independently developed a wearable EEG–EOG acquisition system based on the ADS1299 and ESP32-S3. The system includes an EEG–EOG acquisition board, a dedicated power board, BLE transmission, and interfaces for real-time monitoring on both smartphone and computer.

My work covered analog front-end design, PCB design, generation of ±2V5 and +3V3 power rails, embedded implementation, Bluetooth communication, and the development of signal-receiving and visualization interfaces. This project gave me end-to-end experience in building a functional electrophysiology monitoring system.

Implantable Brain–Computer Interface Structure Fabrication

Research Project
Prototype fabrication, process optimization, and structural exploration

I contributed to the structural fabrication of an implantable brain–computer interface designed for neural recording over a large spatial range using magnetically guided electrode implantation. My work focused on PDMS-based guide structures used as an early prototype, magnetic components, and fabrication methods for producing reproducible guide channels.

I improved the PDMS perforation process by introducing needle insertion before curing to obtain smoother and more consistent channels, and I developed template-based fixation methods to support batch fabrication. I also explored the use of micro-stereolithography to fabricate tent-shaped electrode scaffolds and investigate additional structural fabrication strategies. This project introduced me to the practical challenges of designing and fabricating interfaces intended to interact with neural tissue.

Electrochemical Sweat Glucose Sensing System

Research Project
Circuit design, prototype hardware, Bluetooth communication, and app-based monitoring

I built and evaluated an enzyme-based electrochemical system for sweat glucose detection, including electrode interfacing, amplification and filtering circuits, microcontroller integration, Bluetooth communication, and a mobile Android app for real-time display.

This project gave me practical experience in translating electrochemical sensing principles into a working electronic system, from signal-conditioning hardware and prototype assembly to wireless data transmission and functional demonstration.

Selected Projects

Additional projects that complement my main research experiences and further reflect my interests in biomedical instrumentation, signal processing, and application-oriented system development.

TCD Signal Analysis for Neurocritical Care Assessment

I worked on the processing and analysis of transcranial Doppler (TCD) signals, including spectrogram construction, feature extraction, and classification of healthy versus ICU-related recordings using both conventional machine learning and deep learning approaches.

ECG Monitoring and Visualization Software

I developed an ECG monitoring interface capable of real-time signal display and basic analysis, including heart rate and rhythm-related measurements. This project reflects my interest in practical biomedical software tools that complement sensing hardware.
