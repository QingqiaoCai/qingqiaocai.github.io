University of Notre Dame · Summer 2026

Wearable Microneedle Bioelectronic System for Continuous Health Monitoring

Visiting Researcher · Advisor: Professor Kaiyu Fu

During my summer research at the University of Notre Dame, I worked on an integrated wearable microneedle bioelectronic platform for continuous health monitoring. My work spanned microneedle fabrication and system-level hardware development, including the architecture and PCB design of a wearable sensing system integrating electrochemical and open-circuit-potential sensing channels, power management, embedded control, and Bluetooth communication.

I also contributed to the software workflow for real-time data acquisition, visualization, wireless communication, and device control. This experience gave me the opportunity to work across multiple layers of a biomedical system and strengthened my interest in developing complete wearable platforms rather than isolated sensing components .

Sun Yat-sen University · 2025–Present

Wearable EEG–EOG Monitoring System for Human–Computer Interaction

Independent Researcher · Advisor: Professor Jiyu Li

I independently developed a wearable electrophysiological acquisition system for simultaneous EEG and EOG monitoring. Based on the ADS1299 and ESP32-S3, I designed and iterated the acquisition hardware, including the analog front end, low-noise power circuitry, PCB layout, and Bluetooth Low Energy communication.

Beyond hardware development, I built the host-side acquisition workflow and am developing a MATLAB pipeline for signal preprocessing, feature extraction, and quantitative analysis. Repeated hardware iterations and systematic debugging trained me to move independently from system architecture and circuit design to implementation and validation .

Sun Yat-sen University · Mar 2025 – May 2025

Implantable Brain–Computer Interface for Large-Scale Neural Recording

Research Team Member · Advisor: Professor Xudong Lin

I contributed to the structural fabrication of an implantable brain–computer interface designed for neural recording over a large spatial range using magnetically guided electrode implantation. My work focused on PDMS-based prototype guide structures, magnetic components, and fabrication approaches for producing reproducible guide channels.

I improved the PDMS perforation process by introducing needle insertion before curing, and developed template-based fixation methods to support batch fabrication. I also explored micro-stereolithography for more complex electrode-supporting structures. This project introduced me to the practical challenges of designing interfaces intended to interact with neural tissue .

Sun Yat-sen University · Oct 2025 – Dec 2025

Electrochemical Sweat Glucose Sensing System

Course Project

In this project, I helped develop an enzyme-based electrochemical system for sweat glucose sensing , covering electrode preparation, electrochemical characterization, signal-conditioning electronics, wireless acquisition, and mobile visualization. My primary responsibility was the sensing hardware, including amplification and current-to-voltage conversion circuitry.

I also fabricated Prussian Blue–modified electrodes with immobilized glucose oxidase, performed chronoamperometric measurements for glucose calibration, and developed a mobile application for real-time visualization. This project helped me understand how sensor chemistry, electronics, and software work together in a complete biosensing system .

Featured Publication

2026 · Soft Science

Self-stratified stretchable passive cooling interface for thermal management of on-skin electronics

Jiahao Sun#, Mingzi Liu#, Cancheng Jiang#, Qingqiao Cai, et al.
Soft Science, 2026, 6, 43

This work presents a self-stratified stretchable passive cooling interface for thermal management of on-skin electronics. By leveraging density-driven separation of Al₂O₃ microparticles and liquid metal within an elastomer, the material supports solar reflection and internal heat dissipation while maintaining high stretchability.

Integrated with a wireless skin-interfaced PPG platform, the SPCI reduced device surface temperature during outdoor operation and helped preserve pulse-wave signal quality. The results demonstrate its potential for long-term wearable electronics and human–machine interfaces .
