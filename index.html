University of Notre Dame · Summer 2026

Wearable Microneedle Bioelectronic System for Continuous Health Monitoring

Visiting Researcher · Advisor: Professor Kaiyu Fu

During my summer research at the University of Notre Dame, I worked on an integrated wearable microneedle bioelectronic platform for continuous health monitoring. My work spanned microneedle fabrication and system-level hardware development, including the architecture and PCB design of a wearable sensing system integrating electrochemical and open-circuit-potential sensing channels, power management, embedded control, and Bluetooth communication.

I also contributed to the software workflow for real-time data acquisition, visualization, wireless communication, and device control. This experience gave me the opportunity to work across multiple layers of a biomedical system—from the sensing interface to electronics and software—and strengthened my interest in developing complete, wearable platforms rather than isolated sensing components.

Sun Yat-sen University · 2025–Present

Wearable EEG–EOG Monitoring System for Human–Computer Interaction

Independent Researcher · Advisor: Professor Jiyu Li

I independently developed a wearable electrophysiological acquisition system for simultaneous EEG and EOG monitoring. Based on the ADS1299 and ESP32-S3, I designed and iterated the acquisition hardware, including the analog front end, low-noise power circuitry, PCB layout, and Bluetooth Low Energy communication for real-time wireless signal transmission.

Beyond hardware development, I built the host-side acquisition workflow and am developing a MATLAB pipeline for signal preprocessing, feature extraction, and quantitative analysis. Repeated hardware iterations and systematic debugging throughout this project trained me to move independently from system architecture and circuit design to implementation, troubleshooting, and signal-level validation.

Sun Yat-sen University · Mar 2025 – May 2025

Implantable Brain–Computer Interface for Large-Scale Neural Recording

Research Team Member · Advisor: Professor Xudong Lin

I contributed to the structural fabrication of an implantable brain–computer interface designed for neural recording over a large spatial range using magnetically guided electrode implantation. My work focused on developing PDMS-based prototype guide structures and magnetic components for early-stage design validation, as well as exploring fabrication methods for producing reproducible guide channels.

I improved the PDMS perforation process by introducing needle insertion before curing to obtain smoother and more consistent channels, and I developed template-based fixation methods to support batch fabrication. I also explored micro-stereolithography as an alternative fabrication approach for more complex electrode-supporting structures, fabricating tent-shaped scaffolds to evaluate the feasibility of different structural designs for large-area neural applications. This project introduced me to the practical challenges of designing and fabricating interfaces intended to interact with neural tissue.

Sun Yat-sen University · Oct 2025 – Dec 2025

Electrochemical Sweat Glucose Sensing System

Course Project

In this project, I helped develop an enzyme-based electrochemical system for sweat glucose sensing, covering the workflow from electrode preparation and electrochemical characterization to signal-conditioning electronics, wireless acquisition, and mobile visualization. My primary responsibility was the sensing hardware, where I designed amplification and current-to-voltage conversion circuits for low-level electrochemical currents and integrated Arduino-based acquisition with Bluetooth transmission.

I also fabricated Prussian Blue–modified electrodes with immobilized glucose oxidase, performed chronoamperometric measurements for glucose calibration, and developed a mobile application for real-time visualization and data logging. This project expanded my experience from electrophysiological signals to biochemical sensing and helped me understand how sensor chemistry, electronics, and software must work together in a complete biosensing system.
